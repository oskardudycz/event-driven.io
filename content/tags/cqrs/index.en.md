description: "CQRS means handling changes and queries separately, nothing more. Articles that clear up the myths and show it in practice, mostly in .NET."
---

Start with the article on facts and myths: it explains what CQRS requires and what it doesn't, like separate databases or Event Sourcing. Others answer practical questions, such as whether a command can return a value, and show CQRS in .NET code. The rest look at the read side and at commands: building read models from events and handling a command that arrives twice. The [CQRS](/en/guides/cqrs/) guide puts the key articles in order.
